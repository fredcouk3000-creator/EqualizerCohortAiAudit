Platform Analysis  //  Form Build Decision
Why Typeform.
And Why It Matters.
Three platforms were evaluated for the EQUALIZ(R) AI Audit. Click each to see the full analysis. The recommendation is Typeform.

Typeform is the right platform for EQUALIZ(R). It is the only option that matches the quality level and positioning of a $1,000 founding cohort without requiring custom development.

The core reason is the one-question-at-a-time interface. When a participant receives a form that shows 20 questions in a scrollable list, it signals a generic, low-effort intake process. When they receive a Typeform, it signals something deliberate and well-designed. That perception matters at this price point.

Conditional logic is clean and reliable. Q5a and Q5b (solo vs. team), and Q15 and Q16 (AI usage follow-ons) all require conditional routing. Typeform handles this natively without workarounds or third-party plugins. Google Forms requires a separate section structure that looks clunky. Involve.me handles it but the interface is heavier to configure.

Zapier integration is direct. Typeform connects to Zapier on all paid plans. A submission triggers the Zapier flow immediately, which fires the Claude API call, generates the deployment brief, sends the email, creates the Airtable record, and triggers the Slack invite. This is the automation chain from the onboarding document. Typeform sits cleanly at the top of it.

On branding: Typeform allows custom background colors, fonts, and button styles on the Basic plan and above. The EQUALIZ(R) navy and teal can be applied directly. The form shown in this presentation reflects how the final build will look.

Verdict
Typeform is the only platform that delivers the full feature set this form requires at an acceptable quality level. The $25/month cost is negligible against the revenue this cohort is designed to generate. Recommended: Typeform Basic plan or above.

Google Forms is free and fast to set up. For internal team surveys or quick signups, it is a reasonable tool. For a $1,000 cohort intake form that is the first branded touchpoint after purchase, it is not the right tool.

The UX problem is fundamental. Google Forms shows all questions in a scrollable list. There is no progress indicator by default, no branded visual experience, and no question-by-question focus. A participant who just paid $1,000 and opens a Google Form will feel a mismatch between the quality of the program they signed up for and the experience of filling out the intake.

Conditional logic is limited. Google Forms handles basic section jumps but does not support the type of fine-grained conditional routing the EQUALIZ(R) audit requires. Q5a and Q5b both need to appear based on a specific Q4 answer. Google Forms would require a workaround that makes the form harder to maintain.

No ranking question type. Q8 and Q13 ask participants to rank their top 3 priorities. Google Forms does not have a native ranking input. The workaround is a multiple choice grid, which is clunky and harder to parse for the deployment brief generation.

Zapier connection goes through Google Sheets, not directly from the form. This adds an extra step to the automation chain and introduces a potential delay in triggering the onboarding sequence.

Branding is not possible. You can change the header color and add a logo image. That is the extent of it. The form will always look like a Google Form.

Verdict
Google Forms is appropriate for testing the question set internally before the platform build. It is not appropriate as the participant-facing intake for a premium cohort program. Not recommended for participant use.

Involve.me is already in use for the AI Leverage Audit funnel, which is the strongest argument in its favor. The team has prior experience with the platform and there is an existing account and workflow. That familiarity reduces setup time.

The feature set is comparable to Typeform. Involve.me supports one-at-a-time question flow, native conditional logic, custom branding, Zapier integration, and ranking inputs. On paper, it can do everything the EQUALIZ(R) audit requires.

The gap is in polish and participant perception. Involve.me is primarily positioned as a lead generation and quiz tool. Its visual output, while customizable, tends toward a marketing-quiz aesthetic rather than a premium program intake aesthetic. Getting it to feel as clean and deliberate as Typeform requires significantly more configuration effort.

The pricing advantage is marginal. Involve.me starts at $19/month vs Typeform at $25/month. For a program targeting $20,000 or more in cohort revenue, this difference is not a meaningful factor in the decision.

When involve.me makes more sense: If the team wants to connect the EQUALIZ(R) AI Audit directly to the existing AI Leverage Audit funnel infrastructure and share automation workflows across both products, involve.me removes the need to manage a second platform. This is a legitimate operational argument for involve.me over Typeform.

Verdict
Involve.me is the second-best option and the right choice only if consolidating platforms is a priority. If the goal is the best possible participant experience with the cleanest automation chain, Typeform is still the stronger call.

Use Typeform for the EQUALIZ(R) AI Audit.
